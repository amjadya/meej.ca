### Stage 2... the most interesting one

It's hard to fit everything in the animation, so I'll expand on what's happening in
stage two here.
The gate capacitance stops charging right at the threshold voltage. This is because
with the newly opened pathway from drain-to-source, a dropping voltage on the drain side forces the current incoming from the pin (through $R_G$) to land on the drain side (only briefly).

In the cards I mentioned that no steady current flows through the gate. Here we can see what the brief current is and why it exists. The gate and the drain form a small capacitor, and a capacitor passes current whenever the voltage across it is changing. During stage two the drain falls from about 25 V to about 10 V in 3.3 µs, so:

$$
I = C_{gd}\,\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta t} \approx 400\,\text{pF} \times \frac{15\,\text{V}}{3.3\,\mu\text{s}} \approx 1.8\,\text{mA}
$$

That's nearly all of the current coming from the pin:

$$
I_\text{pin} = \frac{V_\text{pin} - V_\text{gate}}{R_G} \approx \frac{5\,\text{V} - 3\,\text{V}}{1\,\text{k}\Omega} = 2\,\text{mA}
$$

With nothing left over to raise the gate, it stays flat. This brief current stops once the drain stops falling.
