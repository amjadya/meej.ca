## What worked

The vision and prediction pipeline is the year's primary achievement. The
stereo camera system triangulates birdie position in real time, and an
Extended Kalman Filter fused with a physics-based aerodynamics model predicts
landing position and time-to-arrival.

Mechanically, the robot is complete, both in design and fabrication, except
for the motors. Electrically, the power system is mostly finished and is
awaiting motors to begin integration testing. The signal system is still
under development.

## What did not

Motor lead times prevented full assembly, so the Year 1 goal of catching a
flying birdie was not met. The primary constraint of Year 1 was motor lead
time, preventing the physical robot from being assembled and tested.

Birdie detection under gym lighting remains unsolved and is the highest-risk
item for Year 2. HSV thresholding requires sufficient colour information to
work, but the short exposures needed to avoid motion blur at high frame rates
produce images too dark for reliable thresholding. There is a narrow
operating window where exposure is high enough for detection but short enough
to avoid blur, and we were unable to find it reliably. Whether background
subtraction can close this gap is unproven.

Robot localization remains an unmanaged risk. Since our stereo vision system is reliable, we decided to pivot to a stereo vision approach, using ArUco markers on the robot. This simplifies the
localization setup, as the birdie tracking system and the robot localization
system can share the same stereo vision hardware and a common coordinate
system.

## Next

The end goal of a fully autonomous robot remains achievable, but is
contingent on resolving birdie detection and robot localization. The team
recommends prioritizing background subtraction as an alternative to HSV
detection before any further integration work. Across all three subsystems,
the core theory is well-developed and individual components are progressing.
The path to a functional system in Year 2 is clear.

Over the next year, rapid development is required for robot motion and
control system development in order for the robot to reach the required
locations to strike the birdie. One key challenge, along with subsystem
optimization, is integrating each subsystem in a manner which fits the time
budget for hitting the birdie. Timing and telemetry will be key
considerations for Year 2.
